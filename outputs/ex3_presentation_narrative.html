Opening

Slide 1. Title (0:30)

Thanks for the time. I'm going to give you one recommendation for FY28, and then show you the win-rate and renewal work it rests on. I'll take about twenty minutes, and I'd rather spend the rest of our time on you pushing on it.

Slide 2. Agenda (0:30)

Three parts. The recommendation first, with owners, a plan and the ways it could be wrong. Then win rate, where the board number doesn't hold up. Then renewals, where the money at risk is. If you only remember one slide, it's the next one.

01. Recommendation

Slide 3. Section divider: The Recommendation (0:15)

Short version: stop funding outbound and put that capacity on the renewals we are about to lose.

Slide 4. The recommendation (1:45)

From November, move every at-risk renewal over $100K to a save pod of a senior AE plus the account's CSM, and fund it by stopping BDR outbound.

The number I'd put in front of the board is 2.2 times. Our FY27 churn forecast is $3.5M in the medium case. All eleven net-new deals we won in FY26 brought in $1.6M of new ARR. We are on track to lose more than twice what new business added last year.

Two findings drive the call. Net-new win rate is flat at 8.1%, not the 19% in the board pack, and BDR outbound won none of its 75 closed net-new deals. On the renewal side, renewals owned by the renewal desk lost 44% of their ARR in FY26; renewals owned by AEs lost 2%.

For scale: if we only hold FY27 churn at FY26's rate of 16.2%, we keep about $0.4M of ARR. That alone is more than a quarter of what net-new added.

Transition: Let me show you each of those three numbers.

Slide 5. Why renewals (1:15)

Left to right. $3.5M is the FY27 medium churn forecast, with a range of $2.5M to $4.8M. $1.6M is the new ARR from FY26's eleven net-new wins at an 8.1% win rate. And 0 of 75 is BDR outbound's net-new record across FY25 and FY26.

The point is where a dollar of effort returns more. Today the bigger, nearer number is on the renewal side, and the motion we are funding for acquisition hasn't produced a net-new win in two years.

Slide 6. Who owns the renewal (1:30)

This is the finding that tells us what to change, not just where. In FY26 the renewal desk lost 44% of the ARR it owned. AE-owned renewals lost 2%.

It holds when you compare like with like. Among renewals whose notes or calls flagged risk, the desk lost 62 of 75 and AEs lost 11 of 31. Among renewals of $50K or more, the desk lost 9 of 18 and AEs lost none of 37.

I want to be straight about the limit. Ownership is inferred from each owner's book, and it may be that the hardest accounts get routed to the desk. That is Risk 1 on slide 11, and the plan has a tripwire for it.

Slide 7. Where the money is (1:15)

Seventeen open renewals over $100K have risk language in the CSM note or Gong call. Together they hold $5.4M. Twelve of them close in Q4, carrying $4.4M, and the desk owns fourteen of the seventeen.

The four largest are on screen, all closing in Q4 and all with the desk. Antares Metals alone is $1.9M, at 34% utilization, with a competitor in the notes. The full list of seventeen is in the analysis files.

That's why the pods start in November: Q4 is where the exposure sits.

Slide 8. Funding (1:00)

This plan needs no new hires. Six senior AEs from the 51-person AE team lead the seventeen pods. Each account's existing CSM joins its pod. BDRs move off outbound and onto booking value reviews for pod accounts.

On budget, the BDR outbound program line, meaning tools, data and sequences, moves to renewals and funds professional-services rescue work on pod accounts. The export doesn't carry dollar amounts for those lines, so Finance confirms the size in week one.

Slide 9. Stop and start (1:15)

Three stops. BDR outbound stops from 1 November, owned by the VP of Marketing. Health stops being used in forecast calls this quarter, owned by the VP of Customer Success: accounts marked At-Risk churned 0 of 36 in FY26. And the blended win rate comes out of the board pack at the next meeting, owned by the CRO.

Two starts. Save pods on the seventeen renewals, live by 15 November, owned jointly by the VP of Sales and the VP of CS. And a weekly risk review of all 143 open renewals with risk language in the notes, owned by RevOps and paid for from the BDR program line.

Slide 10. 90 days and one year (1:15)

The next 90 days are FY27 Q4: pods live by 15 November, the budget moved, and a target of Q4 churn at or below $1.4M, which is our low case, against a medium forecast of $2.1M.

Then a quarter at a time. In FY28 Q1, desk renewals over $50K move to AE plus CSM coverage, and we turn on stage history and required loss reasons. In Q2, the same six AEs work the $1.8M pursuable expansion list with a target of $0.5M booked. In Q3, a partner-sourced acquisition pilot runs from what's left of the BDR line, aiming for a 10% net-new win rate. By Q4, FY28 ARR churn is at or below 16%, against 18.4% forecast for FY27, and we decide on the future of the desk model.

Slide 11. Two ways this could be wrong (1:30)

Risk 1: ownership tracks risk rather than causing it. If the desk simply gets the accounts that were going to churn, moving them to AEs won't help. Tripwire: if pods retain under 40% of at-risk ARR by 31 January, it's tripped. For reference, in FY26 the desk kept 17% of that ARR and AEs kept 65%. We'd see it in the weekly RevOps save report. If it trips, we revert the pods and keep the weekly triage.

Risk 2: the notes overstate risk months ahead of the renewal. Tripwire: if Q4 at-risk renewals lose under 15% of their ARR by 31 January, against a model range of 25% to 53%, the text signal is too noisy. If it trips, we scale the pods back.

Both are visible inside one quarter, which is the point of starting in November.

Transition: Now the evidence behind the first claim, that win rate didn't double.

02. Win rate

Slide 12. Section divider: Win rate (0:10)

The question was whether new-business win rate doubled, and what drove it.

Slide 13. The board number vs net-new (1:00)

It didn't double. On the board's definition, every New Opportunity record, the rate goes from 9.4% to 18.5%, which is the 9 to 19 in the pack. But 18 of the 36 FY26 wins are order entries and one is an admin record. Counting net-new clients only, the rate is 7.0% in FY25 and 8.1% in FY26: 11 won and 124 lost. That's the number the CRO should see. The definitions and row counts are on the workbook's Definitions tab.

Slide 14. Mix vs performance (1:00)

Splitting the 9-point change: mix explains +10.1 points and performance explains minus 1.1. Order entries and admin records added 8.4 points on their own, and fewer duplicate records added 0.7. Within real deal types, Initial Deployment improved 0.6 points and Expansion fell from 37% to 23%. The parts sum to the total. For the CRO: no sales motion improved.

Slide 15. Segments against plan (0:50)

Enterprise converts best, at 9.8% in FY26, still under its 15% planning assumption. Mid-Market is the miss: 6.8% against a 35% plan, 28 points short. SME is 8.2% against 10%. I've treated SMB and SME as one tier, because the SME label only appears on Sales-sourced rows. The plan rates are a comparison only; nothing in my numbers uses them as inputs.

Slide 16. Sizing the mix lever (0:40)

Shifting mix toward Enterprise is the obvious lever, and it's small. Moving 10 points of closed deals from Mid-Market to Enterprise lifts the blended rate from 8.1% to 8.4%. That's about 0.4 more wins a year, or $57K of new ARR at $144K a win. Even an all-Enterprise mix only reaches 9.8%. That's why the recommendation goes to renewals.

Slide 17. What the data can't tell us (0:50)

Two gaps. First, whether FY26 is final: 44 net-new deals are still open past their close date. If they're dead, FY26 is 6.1%, not 8.1%. Stage and close-date history plus last activity would settle it; RevOps owns that for FY27 Q4.

Second, why deals are won or lost: 58% of losses say No Decision. We can see BDR at 0 of 75 and ecosystem-sourced deals at 14%, but not why. Campaign touches and a required loss reason close that gap; RevOps, FY28 Q1.

Transition: Now the renewal side, where the forecast comes from.

03. Renewals

Slide 18. Section divider: Renewals (0:10)

How much renewal ARR is at risk in FY27, and where.

Slide 19. FY26 baseline (0:50)

FY26 lost 24.1% of renewing logos, 74 of 307 accounts, but only 16.2% of renewing ARR, $2.1M of $13.2M. We lose more small accounts than large ones. On the upside, FY27 renewals over their licence count size to $2.5M of expansion ARR across 826 seats, using the brief's method. I'd pursue $1.8M of it and leave out $0.7M that sits on accounts already showing risk.

Slide 20. FY27 churn forecast (1:00)

FY27 churn falls between $2.5M and $4.8M, with $3.5M as the medium, or 18.4% of renewal ARR due. The model applies each open renewal's FY26 churn rate for its risk group, based on what the notes say and how much of the product is used, and adds Q1 actuals. Q4 carries most of it: $2.1M in the medium case.

Where it's weakest: Q4 renewals with risk language but healthy usage. That group's 25% rate rests on 20 FY26 records, so it's the number I'd watch first.

Slide 21. When the notes and the fields disagree (0:50)

Three named accounts show why the text decides. Hawkridge Co. has utilization of 2.89, which sizes a $489K expansion, but the CSM says usage has plateaued: that's a downsell risk, so it comes off the expansion list. Meridian Foods shows utilization of 0.05, but the notes say adoption is trending up. Jasperton Mechanical carried a Critical flag that was stale, and it renewed.

The rule: the text decides whether there's risk, utilization sets the odds, and Health never breaks a tie.
